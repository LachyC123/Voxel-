# Game design

## Player fantasy
Become the officer who knows every alley and eccentric resident in a small town, handles increasingly chaotic situations, and earns the tools and responsibility to manage larger jobs.

Three pillars:
1. A living town with useful interiors and recurring residents.
2. Responsive physical comedy with understandable cause and effect.
3. Meaningful career progression through varied, well-resolved incidents.

Avoid: endless shooting galleries; aimless NPC wandering; empty enterable boxes; noisy mobile-style HUD; constant ragdoll flopping; XP earned by provoking citizens.

## Core loop
Briefing and loadout → patrol and observe → choose a dispatch call or discover an incident → assess and intervene → follow consequences → resolve and record → return for review and progression.

Target a 15–25 minute shift with a shorter 5–8 minute first shift. These are pacing goals, not timers that forcibly end an active chase. Offer finish-current-incident before clocking off.

## First ten minutes
The player starts in the locker room, equips a torch and radio, and walks through an active station. A short briefing introduces a parking complaint near the shop. The player learns to talk, inspect and identify people. A separate shoplifting incident develops nearby; responding is the first meaningful choice. A short chase can run through the shop, back alley and garden. Recover goods, speak to the owner and finish the shift. Show the next unlock and retain resident memory.

## Player actions
Walk, jog, sprint, crouch, interact, speak, observe, inspect, equip, aim/use tool, collect evidence, request backup, escort and enter/exit vehicles when unlocked.

Suggested bindings: WASD movement; mouse camera; Shift sprint; E contextual action; Tab equipment/case notebook; 1–5 belt slots; R radio menu; Escape pause. Tool aim/use uses mouse buttons. Bindings must be remappable. Reserve or revise keys when adding vehicles; never overload a dialogue confirmation with weapon use.

Context targeting prioritises the thing the player is looking at, with range, line-of-sight and sensible action filtering. Show the target name and action before input. Never arrest or fire because a contextual prompt silently changed.

## Conversation and resolution
Use short authored choices based on known facts: introduce yourself, ask what happened, ask about an item, request cooperation, explain next action. NPC responses depend on belief, personality, relationship and current stress.

Possible endings include reassurance, warning, recovered property, mediated disagreement, arrest, medical handover, referral, unresolved case and suspect escape. Leaving a scene does not magically resolve it. A call can remain pending or be handed to backup.

Arrest is a multi-stage state change: decision and reason → attempt → compliance/resistance response → restrained state → escort/handover. Restrained NPCs cannot spontaneously attack because a generic combat routine fired. Allow cancellation and recovery if an exit is blocked.

## Consequences and fairness
Assessment should use what the player could reasonably know. Never punish a player for hidden scripted facts. Show evidence and a clear explanation of penalties. Property damage, reckless tool use and ignoring obvious injuries affect review; an unavoidable physics glitch must not become permanent career punishment.

Track health/injury, stamina and public trust in simple readable terms. Avoid survival hunger meters. If incapacitated, recover at station or reload the latest safe checkpoint; retain a clear shift outcome. Provide retry for tutorial incidents.

## Replayability
Vary resident roles, locations, motives, evidence and escape choices within validated templates. Repeated personalities and persistent town consequences create continuity. Difficulty increases through competing information and coordinated incidents, not only tougher enemies.

The player may decline a major assignment and run ordinary patrol. Keep minor calls useful at higher ranks through reputation, case leads and resident relationships.

## Companion officer
Later milestone: one partner can follow, hold position, speak to a witness, escort a cooperating detainee and call for help. Give them a limited personality and relevant observations. They must not solve every case, block doors, or constantly speak over residents.

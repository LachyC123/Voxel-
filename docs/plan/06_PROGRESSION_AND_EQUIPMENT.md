# Career, equipment and rewards

## Progression structure
Career XP unlocks rank eligibility. Training unlocks capabilities. Department requisition credits unlock owned equipment choices. Public trust affects cooperation and review. Keep these distinct, with clear labels.

Do not sell mandatory baseline tools back to the player after failure. Department equipment is not purchased with confiscated money. Avoid random weapon loot or rewards per shot.

## Proposed ladder
| Stage | Requirement style | Unlock |
|---|---|---|
| Probationary officer | Complete guided shift | Torch, radio, notebook, basic restraint interaction |
| Patrol officer | Resolve varied calls correctly | Independent shifts, evidence kit, vehicle training |
| Experienced officer | Casework and practical assessments | Better loadout options, partner, linked investigations |
| Specialist-qualified | Complete branch training | Traffic tools, investigative equipment, or high-risk assignments |
| Sergeant | Demonstrate judgement across major jobs | Limited operation planning and backup coordination |

Ranks are fictional game abstractions. A promotion does not automatically grant all weapons. Ordinary patrol remains rewarding at every stage.

## Sample initial tuning
Tutorial completion: 100 career XP and 1 training credit.
Routine call: base 40 XP; up to 20 for useful evidence or successful de-escalation.
Linked case: base 120 XP; bonus tied to supported outcomes.
First patrol eligibility: approximately 300 XP across at least three distinct objectives.
These values are placeholders requiring playtests, not a balanced economy.

Award once per outcome using stable reward transaction IDs. Apply readable penalties with reason and cap. Do not deduct career progress for hidden simulator bugs. Include a development reset and bug-report snapshot.

## Equipment catalogue
| Item | Function | Upgrade choice |
|---|---|---|
| Basic torch | Interior search | Wider beam versus longer reach |
| Radio | Dispatch and backup | More coordination options, not magical faster officers |
| Notebook | Case facts and witness records | Organisation and linked-case access |
| Evidence camera | Record supported observations | Better tagging and retrieval workflow |
| Evidence kit | Collect designated evidence | More carried capacity |
| Restraints | Secure a valid cooperating/subdued target | Reliability/interaction refinements, no arbitrary instant capture |
| Medical pouch | Stabilise supported minor injury states | More uses; specialist help still matters |
| Protective vest | Mitigate defined incoming damage | Protection versus mobility trade-off |
| Traffic cones | Mark a temporary hazard | Larger vehicle-stored set |
| Specialist less-lethal tool | Controlled game combat option | Training and relevant assignment access |
| Sidearm | Later authorised high-risk assignments | Handling options rather than exaggerated damage tiers |
| Long gun | Optional late specialist content | Assignment-specific access; defer until core game works |

Weapons use clear equip, ready, aim, discharge, reload and holster states. Hits interact with the same reaction system. Do not add firearms before basic interactions and encounter judgement work. This is a game equipment specification, not real-world training.

## Loadout and storage
Five quick belt slots initially, with labelled compatible equipment. Larger gear lives in vehicle storage. A station locker is the preparation screen and physical interaction point.
Show a voxel item icon, name, role, current condition/charges where relevant, and assignment restrictions. Unlock preview must explain how to earn it.
Cosmetics can include uniforms, jacket variants, badges and locker decoration. They should not alter NPC guilt or hidden difficulty.

## Vehicles
First vehicle: compact patrol car with forgiving arcade handling, readable braking and limited storage. Add siren/light controls, safe exit placement and seat occupancy. Simulate pedestrians yielding rather than turning every road into unavoidable collisions.
Later: van for capacity; faster response car with handling trade-offs. Build one working vehicle before variants. Do not begin with complex wheel-by-wheel simulation if simpler handling meets the feel.

## Shift review
Display incidents resolved, open leads, recovered property, public feedback, injuries/property damage with context, XP, training progress and next unlock. Offer continue patrol, return to locker or quit safely.
Use short feedback sentences: “Recovered the correct bicycle after checking the frame description.” Make good judgement visible and satisfying.
